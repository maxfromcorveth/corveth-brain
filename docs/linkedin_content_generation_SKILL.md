## Part 2: Voice and Tone

### ALWAYS
- Lowercase throughout. Capitalize proper nouns only (Corveth, Thailand, BJJ).
- Short sentences. Paragraphs of 1-3 lines max.
- Opinions before explanations. Lead with the take, then back it up.
- Casual punctuation. Commas over semicolons. Dashes for emphasis.
- Write like a DM to a smart friend.
- Comfortable saying "I don't know" or "I was wrong."
- One idea per post. Never combine two topics.
- Under 150 words for standard posts. Max 200 for story posts.

### NEVER
- Never start a post with "I" — start with an observation, opinion, or situation.
- Never use "excited to announce", "thrilled to share", "leverage", "synergy", "game-changer."
- Never use engagement bait ("agree?", "comment below", "what do you think?", "repost if...").
- Never write listicles ("5 things I learned from...", "7 frameworks for...").
- Never humble-brag ("so grateful to announce...", "humbled to be...").
- Never use hashtags unless Max specifically asks.
- Never use emojis in the body. One at most, only if natural.
- Never post motivational quotes.
- Never sound like AI generated it. If it sounds polished, rewrite it rougher.
- Never end with a CTA to engage with the post. The CTA is invisible — it's the reader going to Max's profile.

### Voice Examples — Right

- "most startups don't have a sales problem. they have a 'nobody built the system' problem."
- "your CRM has 10,000 contacts and your sales team can't tell you which 50 are worth calling this week. that's not a data problem. that's a decision problem."
- "moved to thailand, started training bjj, and realized getting choked out daily is good prep for client calls."
- "I spent 2 years writing copy for crypto protocols. the ones that survived the bear market weren't the loudest. they were the clearest."
- "hired 3 SDRs before building the system they'd run. fired all 3 within 6 months. the system wasn't the problem. the lack of one was."

### Voice Examples — Wrong

- "Excited to share that Corveth has been named..."
- "Here are 7 frameworks for building a sales pipeline..."
- "As a founder, I believe that resilience is the key to success."
- "Great meeting with the team today! So inspired by everyone's energy!"
- "If you're struggling with your CRM, drop a comment and I'll help you out!"

## Part 3: The Lead Generation Engine

### How Content Converts to Pipeline

```
POST
  → Right reader sees it
    → Recognizes their own pain ("this is about me")
      → Checks Max's profile
        → Sees Corveth, sees credibility
          → DMs Max or books a call
```

This funnel is invisible. The reader should never feel sold to. They should feel understood. The sale happens because they trust Max before the first conversation — not because a post told them to "book a call."

### The Buyer Journey on LinkedIn

```
STRANGER   → sees 1-2 posts   → "interesting take"
FOLLOWER   → sees 5-10 posts  → "this guy knows revenue ops"
WARM LEAD  → sees 15-20 posts → "I have this exact problem"  
INBOUND    → DMs Max          → "can we talk about what you do?"
```

Every single post must serve ALL stages simultaneously. A post should hook strangers AND reinforce credibility for followers AND trigger action from warm leads. If it only serves one stage, it's a wasted post.

## Part 4: Audience Targeting Split

### 80% of Content → Bottom-Funnel (Problem-Aware + Solution-Aware)

**Who they are:** Tech founders and revenue leaders who KNOW their sales system is broken AND KNOW they need someone to fix it. They're comparing options, procrastinating, or convinced they can DIY it. They've probably talked to a consultant before and been burned.

**What they're thinking:**
- "Our CRM is a mess but I don't trust anyone to fix it"
- "We hired SDRs and it didn't work, maybe we need infrastructure first"
- "Every consultant gives me a deck and disappears"
- "I know I need help but I don't want to waste money on another audit"
- "Is this something I should just figure out myself?"

**Content goal:** Make them feel deeply seen. Make Max the obvious and only choice. Remove every objection before they even raise it.

**What this content looks like:**
- Naming specific pain so precisely the reader screenshots it and sends it to their co-founder
- Showing how Max solved this exact problem (proof, not theory)
- Contrarian takes that differentiate Corveth from every other consultant
- "Behind the scenes" of actual client work (anonymized) showing the messy reality
- Direct attacks on the broken alternatives (agencies, premature hiring, DIY)

### 20% of Content → Mid-Funnel (Problem-Aware, NOT Solution-Aware)

**Who they are:** Tech founders who feel the pain but haven't connected it to "I need revenue operations." They think it's a hiring problem, a tool problem, or a market problem. They don't know what RevOps is or why it matters.

**What they're thinking:**
- "We're not closing deals but I think we just need better salespeople"
- "Our HubSpot isn't working, maybe we should switch to Salesforce"
- "Marketing is generating leads but nothing converts"
- "I don't even know what revenue operations means"

**Content goal:** Name their pain for them. Reframe their problem from "I need better salespeople" to "I need a system before I hire anyone." Move them from problem-aware to solution-aware over time.

**What this content looks like:**
- Educational takes that reframe common beliefs ("you don't have a sales problem, you have a systems problem")
- Simple explanations of what RevOps actually means in practice
- "Before and after" scenarios showing what changes when you build the system first
- Myth-busting content ("switching CRMs won't fix your pipeline")

## Part 5: The Persuasion Framework

Every post should activate at least one of these five psychological drivers. The best posts hit 2-3 simultaneously. These are not templates — they're lenses to write through.

### 1. Encourage Their Dreams

**The dream:** A sales engine that runs predictably. Revenue they can forecast. A system that works so they can focus on product instead of chasing deals.

**How to use it:** Paint the picture of what life looks like AFTER the system is built. Not with hype — with specifics. Make the dream feel achievable, not aspirational.

**Examples:**
- "imagine knowing on monday morning exactly how many deals will close this month. not hoping. knowing. that's what a real pipeline does."
- "the goal isn't more leads. the goal is knowing which 10 conversations matter this week and ignoring the rest."
- "the founders who sleep well aren't the ones with the most pipeline. they're the ones who can actually read their pipeline."

### 2. Justify Their Failures

**The failure they feel:** They tried to build sales and it didn't work. They hired SDRs too early. They bought tools that sit unused. They feel like they should've figured this out by now.

**How to use it:** Normalize the failure. Make it systemic, not personal. Show that every technical founder faces this because nobody teaches it.

**Examples:**
- "you didn't fail at sales. you were never given the infrastructure to succeed at it. there's a difference."
- "every technical founder I've worked with tried to DIY their sales system. every single one. it's not a character flaw — it's what builders do. they try to build everything themselves."
- "your SDRs didn't fail because they were bad. they failed because there was no system underneath them. you sent soldiers into battle without a map."

### 3. Allay Their Fears

**Their fears:** Wasting money on another consultant who delivers nothing. Losing control of their sales process. Being sold something they don't need. Looking stupid for not knowing how to sell.

**How to use it:** Address the fear directly without being salesy about it. Show that Max understands WHY they're skeptical and that the skepticism is valid.

**Examples:**
- "the best sales system in the world is useless if the founder doesn't understand how it works. that's why I don't build black boxes. I build something you could run without me tomorrow."
- "if a consultant hands you a 50-page PDF and calls it a strategy, run. strategy without implementation is just a bill."
- "you don't need to become a salesperson. you need a system that lets you sell without pretending to be something you're not."

### 4. Confirm Their Suspicions

**Their suspicions:** Most sales consultants are full of shit. The big agency model is broken. Hiring SDRs before building infrastructure is backwards. Their gut feeling that something is fundamentally wrong with how they're doing sales is correct.

**How to use it:** Validate what they already believe but haven't heard anyone say out loud. Be the person who finally says the thing they've been thinking.

**Examples:**
- "your gut was right. hiring 3 SDRs before you had a defined ICP, a working CRM, and a tested sequence was always going to fail. everyone told you to 'just hire salespeople.' everyone was wrong."
- "most sales consultants sell you what they know, not what you need. if all they know is HubSpot, guess what they'll recommend."
- "the agency charged you $15k/month and sent you a dashboard full of vanity metrics. you suspected it was bullshit. it was."

### 5. Help Them Throw Rocks at Their Enemies

**Their enemies:** Consultants who ghost after the audit. Agencies that optimize for their own retainer. SDR-first culture that wastes money. CRM vendors who oversell and underdeliver. The "just hire salespeople" advice that everyone gives.

**How to use it:** Give them language and ammunition to articulate what's wrong with the status quo. Position Corveth as the antidote without directly pitching.

**Examples:**
- "the consulting industry's business model is to make you dependent. deliver an audit, create confusion, sell the implementation. Corveth's model is the opposite: build it, teach it, leave."
- "every CRM vendor will tell you their tool is the answer. none of them will tell you the tool is 10% of the problem. the other 90% is process, data architecture, and team adoption."
- "'just hire more SDRs' is the 'just raise more money' of sales advice. it sounds smart. it's expensive. it doesn't solve the actual problem."

## Part 6: Post Formats

### Format 1: Short Take (3-5 lines)
A single opinion or observation. No setup, just the punch. Best for confirming suspicions and throwing rocks.

**Template:**
```
[bold opinion or observation in 1-2 lines]

[1-2 lines of supporting logic or proof]
```

**Example:**
```
your CRM has 10,000 contacts and your sales team can't tell you which 50 are worth calling this week.

that's not a data problem. that's a decision problem. and no tool upgrade will fix it.
```

### Format 2: Story Post (8-15 lines)
A real experience with a lesson baked in. Start with the situation, end with the insight. Never spell out the moral. Best for demonstrating competence and justifying failures.

**Template:**
```
[situation — what happened, 2-3 lines]

[tension — what went wrong or what was surprising, 3-4 lines]

[resolution — what changed, 2-3 lines]

[insight — the takeaway, 1-2 lines. never start with "the lesson here is..."]
```

**Example:**
```
last month a founder told me his sales team sends 200 cold emails a day and books 2 meetings a month.

I asked to see the emails. first line of every single one: "I hope this finds you well." the ICP definition was "anyone in fintech." the CRM had no lead scoring, no segmentation, no follow-up triggers. the team was spraying into the void.

we rewrote 3 templates, tightened the ICP to 40 companies, and built a 3-step sequence with actual personalization. 11 meetings booked in the first week.

same list. same team. same tool. the bottleneck is almost never where founders think it is.
```

### Format 3: Contrarian Take (5-8 lines)
Challenge a common belief. Not for controversy — because Max genuinely sees it differently. Best for confirming suspicions and educating mid-funnel readers.

**Template:**
```
[the common belief, stated simply]

[why it's wrong, 2-3 lines]

[what's actually true, 2-3 lines]
```

**Example:**
```
unpopular opinion: most startups don't need a CRM until they have 50+ active conversations.

before that, a spreadsheet and discipline beats any $50k hubspot setup. I've seen seed-stage founders spend 3 months configuring a CRM they'll use for 20 contacts. that's not building a sales system. that's procrastinating on selling.

the tool isn't the system. the system is the thinking behind it. get the thinking right first, then buy the tool.
```

### Format 4: Personal + Professional Crossover (5-10 lines)
Connect a personal experience to a business insight naturally. NOT forced. Best for creating preference and making people like Max as a person.

**Template:**
```
[personal moment — bjj, thailand, food, travel, 2-3 lines]

[natural bridge — NOT "and that's just like in business..." — 1 line]

[professional insight that emerged from the personal moment, 2-3 lines]
```

**Example:**
```
got submitted 4 times in a row at bjj this morning by someone 15kg lighter than me.

technique beats size every time.

same thing happens in sales. the team with 3 reps and a clean system outperforms the team with 12 reps and chaos. every time. headcount is not a strategy.
```

### Format 5: Proof Post (5-10 lines)
Show a result without pitching. Anonymized client work, before/after, or a specific metric. Best for encouraging dreams and demonstrating competence.

**Template:**
```
[the situation before — 2-3 lines]

[what was done — 2-3 lines, specific but not detailed enough to replicate]

[the result — 1-2 lines, specific metric]

[no CTA, no "DM me if you want the same." just end.]
```

**Example:**
```
a fintech startup came to us forecasting "maybe $200k this quarter." their pipeline had 400 "leads" with no scoring, no stages, no close dates.

we rebuilt the pipeline from scratch. defined 5 stages with clear entry criteria. killed 340 dead leads. added close date discipline and weekly pipeline reviews.

next quarter they forecasted $380k. they closed $410k. first accurate forecast in the company's history.
```

### Format 6: Photo + Short Caption (1-3 lines)
A real photo from daily life with a short caption. Not staged. Best for creating preference and showing the human behind the brand.

**Example:**
```
[photo of laptop at a thai cafe]

this is where your CRM architecture gets designed. zero complaints.
```

## Part 9: Quality Gates

Before publishing ANY post, run through this checklist:

### Gate 1: Does It Serve the Funnel?
- [ ] Would a CEO of a Series A fintech startup read this and think "this is about me"?
- [ ] Does it surface a pain, demonstrate competence, or create preference?
- [ ] Does it activate at least one of the 5 persuasion drivers?
- [ ] Is it targeting the right funnel stage (80% bottom, 20% mid)?

### Gate 2: Does It Sound Like Max?
- [ ] Is it lowercase?
- [ ] Does it start with an observation or opinion, NOT with "I"?
- [ ] Is it under 150 words (200 for story posts)?
- [ ] Would Max actually say this in a conversation?
- [ ] Does it sound rough and real, not polished and corporate?

### Gate 3: Is It NOT Doing These Things?
- [ ] No engagement bait at the end
- [ ] No hashtags
- [ ] No emoji spam
- [ ] No listicle format
- [ ] No humble-bragging
- [ ] No motivational quotes
- [ ] No explicit pitch or CTA to book a call

### Gate 4: The Screenshot Test
- [ ] Would someone screenshot this and send it to their co-founder with "this is literally us"?
- [ ] If not, rewrite until they would.

## Part 10: Anti-Patterns — What Kills Lead Gen Content

### 1. Engagement Farming
Writing for likes and comments instead of for your buyer. Signs: "agree?", polls about obvious things, posting about trending topics with no connection to RevOps, tagging random people for visibility.

**Why it kills leads:** Your buyer is a busy CEO. They don't comment on posts. They lurk, they read, they DM when ready. Optimizing for comments optimizes for the wrong audience.

### 2. Generic Advice
"Focus on your ICP." "Build a sales process." "Hire A-players." Content that is true but useless because everyone says it.

**Why it kills leads:** Generic advice positions Max as one of 10,000 people saying the same thing. The buyer can't distinguish Max from anyone else. Specificity is what creates trust.

### 3. Theory Without Implementation
Explaining frameworks, models, and concepts without showing what it looks like in practice.

**Why it kills leads:** Max's entire positioning is "we implement, not audit." Content that stays at the theory level contradicts the brand. Always show the messy, practical reality.

### 4. Virtue Signaling
"I always put my clients first." "Transparency is my core value." "I believe in doing the right thing."

**Why it kills leads:** Nobody believes it. It sounds like every other consultant. Max's credibility comes from showing the work, not claiming values.

### 5. Overproduction
Polished graphics, designed carousels, professional headshot aesthetics. Looking like a personal brand factory.

**Why it kills leads:** Max's buyers are technical founders. They distrust anything that looks too marketed. Raw text posts with real stories outperform designed content for this audience every time.

## Part 11: Content Generation Process

When Max requests a LinkedIn post, follow this process:

### Step 1: Classify the Request
- What pillar? (Revenue ops, founder life, personal)
- What funnel stage? (Bottom 80% or mid 20%)
- What persuasion driver fits best?

### Step 2: Choose a Format
- Short take, story, contrarian, crossover, proof, or photo caption
- Match format to the idea — don't force a story into a short take

### Step 3: Draft
- Write the post following voice and tone rules
- Hit the persuasion driver
- Keep under word limit
- Start strong — first line must stop the scroll

### Step 4: Quality Gate
- Run through all 4 gates
- The screenshot test is the most important one
- If it doesn't pass, rewrite — don't patch

### Step 5: Deliver
- Provide 2-3 variations if the idea supports it
- Flag which persuasion driver each variation hits
- Flag which funnel stage it targets
- Let Max choose

## Part 12: First Line Library

The first line of a LinkedIn post determines if anyone reads the rest. Here are patterns that work for Max's audience. Use these as inspiration, not as templates to copy.

### Pain-Naming Openers
- "your sales team is busy. your pipeline is empty. those two things are connected."
- "3 SDRs, 2 tools, 0 system. that's not a sales team. that's organized chaos."

- "a crypto startup called me after burning through $80k on a sales agency in 4 months. zero closed deals."
- "got on a call with a CTO who told me 'we don't have a sales problem.' their last 3 quarters missed forecast by 40%."

- "there's one question I ask every founder in the first 5 minutes. most can't answer it."
- "the difference between a $200k quarter and a $400k quarter wasn't more leads."
- "every startup I've worked with made the same mistake before calling me."
