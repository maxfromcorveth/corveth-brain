## Purpose

Generate LinkedIn posts that make the right person think “I need to talk to this guy.” This is NOT engagement farming. The metric that matters: did a potential buyer DM Max or book a call because of this post?

Every post is a nurture touchpoint. Content IS the lead nurture system. Each post must move someone closer to reaching out — or it doesn’t get published.

## North Star: Carlo Acutis

Carlo Acutis used technology to serve truth and others — not to promote himself. He cataloged Eucharistic miracles online because the information served people, not because it built his brand. This is the spirit behind every post:

- **Serve before you extract.** Give the reader something useful before you ask for anything.
- **Humility in expertise.** Share what you know because it helps, not because it makes you look smart.
- **Truth over performance.** Never exaggerate results, fabricate stories, or manipulate. Let the work speak.
- **Generosity as strategy.** The more you give away, the more they trust you. Trust converts.

If a post wouldn’t pass the Carlo test — “does this serve the reader more than it serves me?” — rewrite it or kill it.

**This is Gate 1. Every post must pass the Carlo test BEFORE any other quality gate is applied. If it fails here, nothing else matters — rewrite or kill it.**

## Part 2: The Audience

### ICP (Ideal Customer Profile)

**Primary definition:** CEO, Founder, or CTO at a pre-seed to Series B tech company selling complex products, where the CRM is broken or nonexistent and rev ops infrastructure needs building.

**Profile detail:** 10-50 employees. They built something impressive but can’t sell it consistently. Their CRM is either empty, a mess, or doesn’t exist at all. They’re considering hiring a head of sales or SDRs but aren’t sure if that’s the right move yet. The CRM state is a defining qualifier — if their sales infrastructure is already functional, they’re not the buyer.

**Decision maker titles:** CEO, Founder, CTO. These are the people who feel the pain directly and have budget authority. CTOs matter because in technical product companies, the CTO often owns or influences the sales tooling stack and process decisions.

**Funding range:** Pre-seed through Series B. Earlier-stage founders (pre-seed, seed) often have zero sales infrastructure and need everything built from scratch. Series A/B founders have tried something that isn’t working and need it rebuilt or systematized.

**The person reading your post:**

- Has a product that works but pipeline that doesn’t
- Has tried agencies, freelancers, or doing it themselves
- Is skeptical of consultants because they’ve been burned
- Respects builders, not talkers
- Makes decisions based on proof, not promises

### The 80/20 Split

**80% of content targets:** People who are BOTH problem-aware AND solution-aware. They know their sales system is broken. They know they need someone to fix it. They’re comparing options or procrastinating. Content goal: make them feel seen, make Max the obvious choice.

**20% of content targets:** People who are problem-aware but NOT solution-aware. They know something is wrong but haven’t connected it to “I need revenue operations.” Content goal: name their pain. Educational but still opinionated. Moves them from problem-aware to solution-aware over time. Longer to convert but grows reach.

### The 5 Persuasion Drivers

Every post should activate at least one of these. The best posts activate 2-3:

1. **Encourage their dreams.** “Imagine a pipeline where every lead is actually qualified.” Paint the vision of what a working sales engine looks like. Make them want it.
2. **Justify their failures.** “You’re a technical founder, not a sales expert. Of course your CRM is a mess.” Remove the shame. It’s not their fault — they were building product, not pipeline.
3. **Allay their fears.** “You don’t need to hire a VP Sales to fix this.” Reduce the perceived risk and cost of solving the problem. Make it feel doable.
4. **Confirm their suspicions.** “That agency sent you a 200-slide deck and zero qualified leads? Yeah, that’s standard.” Validate what they already suspect but haven’t heard anyone say out loud.
5. **Help them throw rocks at enemies.** “Most sales consultants deliver a PDF and ghost.” Give them a common enemy. Bloated agencies. Premature SDR hires. CRM vendors who sell complexity.

## Part 3: Research Layer

Before writing anything, know what your ICP is saying RIGHT NOW. Posts built on assumptions underperform. Posts built on real language from real founders stop the scroll.

### Weekly Research Routine (15-20 minutes, before writing the week’s batch)

**Source 1: Reddit (r/sales, r/startups, r/SaaS)**

- Search for: “outbound,” “pipeline,” “CRM,” “sales hire,” “lead gen,” “cold email”
- Sort by: new or top (last 7 days)
- Capture: exact phrases founders use to describe their pain. These become hooks.
- Example find: “we’ve burned through 3 SDRs and still can’t get consistent pipeline” → Mirror & Redirect post.

**Source 2: LinkedIn comments and posts from ICP**

- Browse posts from founders, CTOs, and VPs Sales in your network
- Look for: complaints, questions, debates, frustrations
- Capture: the specific language they use — not your rewrite of it
- Example find: a founder commenting “honestly our CRM is just a graveyard at this point” → direct hook material

**Source 3: X/Twitter**

- Search for: “sales process,” “hiring SDR,” “outbound is dead,” “pipeline problem”
- Look for: hot takes getting engagement, debates between founders and sales leaders
- Capture: contrarian angles you can respond to from experience

**Source 4: Client/lead conversations**

- Review DM conversations, Telegram threads, call notes from the past week
- Capture: exact questions prospects asked, objections they raised, language they used
- This is the highest-signal source — real people describing real problems to you directly

### How to Use What You Find

1. **Hooks:** Use exact ICP language as Mirror & Redirect openers or story post hooks
2. **Angles:** If a topic is generating debate on Reddit/X, write your take from experience
3. **Proof posts:** If a prospect described a problem you solved, anonymize it and write the case study
4. **Framework posts:** If you see the same question asked 3+ times, teach the answer

### Storage

Keep a running note (phone or doc) called “ICP voice capture.” When you see a phrase, complaint, or question from a founder that resonates — paste it in. When it’s time to write, scan the list first. Start from their words, not yours.

### Rule

If you haven’t done the research scan this week, don’t write the batch. Stale angles = low impressions. Fresh ICP language = better hooks.

## Part 5: Post Formats

### Format 1: Mirror & Redirect (PRIMARY — use 1x/week max)

The top-performing format. 3,982 impressions on first use (vs. avg ~250 for other formats).

**Structure:** Quote what founders actually say (the mirror), then redirect to the real issue.

**Template:**

```
"[exact thing a founder says]"

[1-2 sentences validating why they think that]

[the redirect — what's actually going on]

[the insight — what they should do instead]
```

**Example:**

```
"we need to hire a head of sales"

maybe. but 8 out of 10 times what you actually need is a system.

a head of sales can't fix what doesn't exist. if there's no pipeline, no scoring, no sequence — you're hiring someone to build from scratch with a six-figure salary.

build the system first. then hire someone to run it.
```

**Why it works:** The reader recognizes their own words. That recognition is the hook. The redirect earns trust because it’s honest, not salesy.

**Rules:**

- Max 1x per week to prevent format fatigue
- The quote must be something a real founder has said or would say
- The redirect must be genuinely useful, not a veiled pitch

### Format 2: Short Take

A punchy opinion in under 100 words. No story, no buildup. Just the take.

**Template:**

```
[bold statement]

[2-3 sentences of reasoning]

[landing line — the "so what"]
```

**Rules:**

- Must pass the Debate Test (Gate 6): would someone disagree with this?
- If no one would disagree, it’s not an opinion — it’s a platitude. Kill it.
- Rotate angles weekly (see Angle Rotation section)

### Format 3: Story Post

A real situation with a real lesson. Under 200 words.

**Template (v3 — adapted from proof capture system):**

```
[the internal struggle — what the founder was feeling]

[the external metrics — what the numbers looked like]

[the skepticism — why they almost didn't act]

[what made them act — the turning point]

[the new metrics — what changed]

[the best moment since — the human payoff]
```

**Rules:**

- Must include at least 2 specific, concrete details the reader wouldn’t guess
- Must include at least 1 hard number
- The founder in the story must look like the ICP (tech founder, pre-seed to Series B, complex product)
- Never use “a client” — use role + company type (“a fintech founder,” “a B2B SaaS CTO”)
- Reference recent work, not timeless anonymized stories
- Any real person who inspired the story should feel helped, not exposed

### Format 4: Contrarian Take

Challenge conventional wisdom. Create productive tension.

**Template:**

```
[the thing everyone believes]

[why it's wrong or incomplete]

[what you've seen work instead]
```

**Rules:**

- Must pass the Debate Test
- Must come from direct experience, not theory (Gate 7: Source Test)
- Rotate the thesis — never repeat the same contrarian angle within 2 weeks

### Format 5: Proof Post / Case Study

The money format. This is where inbound leads come from.

**Proof Hierarchy (use as quality checklist — higher = better):**

1. Show > Tell — screenshots, before/after data, dashboard photos (anonymized)
2. Raw > Processed — real Slack messages, real Attio metrics, not cleaned-up narratives
3. Personal > Generic — specific pain details the reader wouldn’t guess
4. Numbers > No Numbers — at least 1 hard number per proof post
5. Others saying it > You saying it — client quotes, comment threads, tagged reactions
6. Identical to ICP > Opposite — feature founders who look like your buyer
7. Newer > Older — reference recent work, not old war stories
8. Match claims to proof — don’t prove your claims; make claims your proof already supports

**Rules:**

- Start from what you can screenshot, then write the post around it
- Every proof post needs a soft CTA (see CTA Rules)
- 2 per week minimum

### Format 6: Framework / How-To Post

Teach a concept or system. Educational but opinionated.

**Template:**

```
[the problem most founders face]

[the framework or system that solves it — described simply]

[one specific example of it working]
```

**Rules:**

- Never credit frameworks to their authors by name
- Never claim ownership either — use “a framework I adapted” or just present it naturally
- Must be actionable — the reader should be able to do something with it today
- 1 per week

### Format 7: Personal Crossover

A personal experience (BJJ, faith, travel, life) bridged to a business insight.

**Strict Gate (v3 — from bouquet analysis):**

Before publishing, ask: “does this pairing strengthen or dilute the bouquet?”

The bouquet = the collection of associations people have with “Max Gandolfo.” Every post adds a flower. If the personal crossover adds a flower your ICP values (discipline, grit, strategic thinking, humility), keep it. If it adds a flower that dilutes (random hobby content, lifestyle flex), kill it.

**Test:** Would this post work as a pure business post without the personal hook? If yes → keep the hook. If no → the personal element is carrying a weak business insight. Kill it.

**Rules:**

- Max 1 per week
- Must have a tight business bridge — not a loose metaphor
- BJJ → discipline, strategy, ego management, competing against bigger opponents = good pairings
- Travel, food, lifestyle without business bridge = bad pairings

## Part 7: CTA Strategy

### Rules by Post Type

The 14-day data showed zero CTAs across 14 posts. Zero conversion path. This section fixes that.

**Case Study / Proof Posts:** Soft CTA. Always.

- “dm me ‘audit’ if you want me to look at yours”
- “dm me ‘pipeline’ and i’ll send you the framework”
- Never hard-sell. Never “book a call.” The CTA is an invitation, not a pitch.

**All other post types:** No CTA. Let the content do the work. The proof posts carry the conversion load.

**Lead Magnet Exchange (from Marketing Machine):**

- Create a one-pager or framework doc
- Offer it in exchange for a DM on case study posts
- When they get value from it, ask for a testimonial
- The testimonial becomes proof for future posts → the loop feeds itself

### DM Response Rules

When someone DMs after a CTA:

- Respond within minutes, not hours (Speed pillar from Lead Nurture)
- Lowercase, casual — like texting a colleague
- Don’t pitch. Ask one short question about their situation.
- Example: “hey — what’s your pipeline look like rn? crm or spreadsheet?”
- Let them talk. Make them feel seen before anything else.

## Part 8: Quality Gates

Every post must pass ALL gates before publishing. **Carlo is Gate 1 — the foundation. If a post fails Gate 1, no other gate matters. Rewrite or kill it.**

**Gate 1 — Carlo Test (FOUNDATION):** Does this serve the reader more than it serves me? Is this generous, truthful, and humble? If the post leans toward self-promotion, personal branding, or making Max look good rather than helping the reader solve a problem — rewrite it or kill it. This gate is not a checkbox. It is the lens through which every other gate is evaluated. A post that passes Gates 2-10 but fails Gate 1 does not get published.

**Gate 2 — ICP Check:** Would my ideal buyer (CEO, Founder, or CTO at a pre-seed to Series B tech company selling complex products, CRM broken or nonexistent) care about this?

**Gate 3 — Voice Check:** Is this lowercase? Under 150w (200 for stories)? No “I” start? No hashtags? No listicles?

**Gate 4 — Persuasion Check:** Does this activate at least 1 of the 5 persuasion drivers?

**Gate 5 — Pairing Check (v3 — from Branding Playbook):** What is this post pairing “Max Gandolfo” with? Does my ICP like that thing? Does it strengthen or dilute the bouquet?

**Gate 6 — Debate Test:** Would someone disagree with this? If no one would argue the other side, it’s a platitude, not an opinion. Kill it.

**Gate 7 — Source Test:** Is this from direct experience or theory? If theory, either find a real example to anchor it or kill it.

**Gate 8 — Authority Level Check (v3):** What authority level is this post?

- Level 1: what you say (opinions, takes) — acceptable but not ideal
- Level 2: what others say about you (client results, testimonials, comment threads from senior people) — target this
- Level 3: what they experience (the reader gets value just from the post itself) — the gold standard

Push for Level 2+ whenever possible. Your top post (3,982 impressions) hit Level 2 accidentally when senior people debated in comments. Make that intentional.

**Gate 9 — Proof Gate (for case study / proof posts only):** Does this post score 5+ on the 8-point proof hierarchy? If not, find stronger proof or don’t publish.

**Gate 10 — Angle Rotation Check:** Has this thesis appeared in the last 2 weeks? If yes, pick a different angle from the Topic Bank.

1. **Client interactions** — screenshot wins from Slack, Attio, Telegram as they happen
2. **Expandi / outbound data** — reply rates, engagement metrics, conversion numbers
3. **CRM snapshots** — before/after pipeline views, lead scoring improvements

### Weekly Capture Sources

1. **LinkedIn comments** — when ICP-matching people validate your takes in comments, screenshot them. These are Level 2 authority proof.
2. **DM conversations** — when someone DMs asking for help, that’s proof of demand (anonymized)

### Monthly Capture Sources

1. **Testimonial requests** — after delivering value (lead magnet, framework doc, free audit), ask for a testimonial using this structure:

- What were you struggling with before?
- What numbers/metrics reflected that struggle?
- What made you skeptical about getting help?

- What changed after? (numbers)
- What’s the best part of having this solved?

### Storage

Tag everything in a proof folder. When it’s time to write a case study post, start from the proof, not from a thesis. Don’t prove your claims — make claims your proof supports.

## Part 10: Content as Lead Nurture

Your LinkedIn feed IS your nurture sequence. The 4 pillars of lead nurture (adapted for content):

### Pillar 1: Availability (Consistency)

Post 5x/week minimum. Be in their feed daily. If you’re not there, a competitor is. Your audience can’t schedule a “meeting” with your content if you’re not posting.

### Pillar 2: Speed (Responsiveness)

When someone engages — likes, comments, DMs — respond within minutes. Not hours. The data shows that responding within 60 seconds creates a 391% increase in conversions vs. waiting. On LinkedIn, speed signals: “this person is real, responsive, and worth talking to.”

When a post generates quality comments, engage every single one. Don’t just “like” their comment — reply with substance. This is free Level 2 authority building.

### Pillar 3: Personalization (Relevance)

Segment your content to speak to your ICP’s specific pain. Use matched proof in case studies (show results from founders who look like your reader). Use their language, not consultant-speak.

When someone DMs, look at their profile for 2 minutes before responding. Mention something specific about their company or situation. This is the “do your homework on the good leads” principle applied to LinkedIn.

### Pillar 4: Volume (Persistence)

Don’t give up after one post on a topic. Work an angle across multiple formats over weeks:

- Week 1: Short take on the problem
- Week 2: Case study showing the solution
- Week 3: Framework post teaching the method
- Week 4: Mirror & Redirect from a founder who had the problem

Same thesis, different format, different angle. This is not repetition — it’s reinforcement. BAMFAM for content: every post should set up the next one.

## Part 11: Hook Rules

### Hook Quality Standards

1. The hook must be about the reader, not about Max. If the first line describes Max’s experience or credentials, rewrite it.
2. Vague enough to stop multiple scrollers, specific enough that the ICP feels targeted. Specificity comes in line 2-3.
3. No format giveaway in the hook. Don’t reveal the topic category in line 1 if it would cause non-obvious audiences to scroll past.
4. One hook per post. Don’t stack.

### Hook Types by Format

|Format            |Best Hook Types         |Why                                                      |
|------------------|------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------|
|Mirror & Redirect |Direct quote            |The quote IS the hook — reader recognizes their own words|
|Short Take        |Statements, Questions   |Punch-first energy                                       |
|Story Post        |Stories, Conditionals   |Drops reader into the scene                              |
|Contrarian Take   |Statements, Exclamations|Bold claim creates tension                               |
|Personal Crossover|Questions, Conditionals |Keeps topic hidden so both audiences stop                |
|Proof Post        |Statements, Stories     |Lead with the before-state or the result                 |

### 70-20-10 Hook Testing

- 70% — hooks that have proven to work for Max’s audience
- 20% — variations on proven hooks (same structure, different subject)
- 10% — experiments (new formats, new angles). If they win, move to 70%. If they lose, document and don’t repeat.

### Hooks That Violate Max’s Voice (Never Use)

- “excited to share…” / “thrilled to announce…”
- “here are 7 ways to…”
- “I’m going to show you…”
- “agree?” / “thoughts?” / “comment below”
- Anything that sounds like it was written by a LinkedIn growth coach

## Part 12: What NOT To Post

- Anything about live negotiations or current client situations that could identify them
- Anything that conflicts with Orthodox Christian values — no manipulation, no deception, no content that tears others down for sport
- Lifestyle flex content (travel, food, possessions) without a tight business bridge
- Pure motivation / hustle porn — “grind harder” posts don’t convert and don’t serve
- Hot takes on trending news unless directly relevant to the ICP’s pain
- Anything where the real person who inspired it would feel exposed rather than helped
- Repeated same-angle posts within a 2-week window
- Posts longer than 200 words
- Content that farms engagement without a path to conversion

## Quick Reference: Content Generation Process

1. **Run the research scan first.** Check Reddit, LinkedIn, X, and client conversations for fresh ICP language. If you haven’t scanned this week, don’t write.
2. **Run Gate 1 (Carlo Test).** Does this post serve the reader more than it serves me? If no — rewrite or kill before going further.
3. Check the angle rotation log. What hasn’t been covered in 2 weeks?
4. Pick a format from the weekly calendar.
5. If it’s a proof post → go to proof folder first. Start from evidence, not thesis.
6. Write the post using fresh ICP language from the research scan. Under 150 words (200 for stories).
7. Run through all remaining quality gates (2-10).
8. If it’s a case study → add soft CTA.
9. Post. Engage with every comment within minutes.
10. Log the post in the tracking sheet.
11. If quality comments appear from ICP-matching people → screenshot for future proof.
