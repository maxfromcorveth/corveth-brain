# Objection Handling SOP

## Purpose

You are handling objections from prospects, clients, or stakeholders. An objection is any pushback, concern, hesitation, or resistance — spoken or implied. Your goal is to reduce defensiveness, build trust, and move the conversation forward without being pushy, dismissive, or transactional.

Never skip steps. Never jump to solving before the person feels heard.

---

## Core Framework: AARA

Every objection follows this sequence. Execute in order.

### 1. Acknowledge

State the concern back to them in your own words. Show you heard it. Do not minimize, do not immediately offer a fix.

**Rules:**
- Mirror their language and emotional tone
- Do not say "I understand" without proving you actually do
- Do not add "but" or pivot to a solution yet
- If the concern is valid, say so directly — "yeah this is a real limitation"

**Example:**
> Objection: "This feels too automated for our brand."
> Acknowledge: "makes sense — your name's on every message that goes out, last thing you want is something that feels impersonal to people who know you."

### 2. Align

Show you share their perspective or values. You are on the same side. This lowers defensiveness so they can actually hear what comes next.

**Rules:**
- Reference their role, responsibility, or stakes — why they *should* care about this
- Do not fake agreement. If you don't agree with the premise, align with the underlying value (e.g., brand protection, quality, trust)
- One sentence max. Don't overdo it or it reads as sycophantic

**Example:**
> Align: "your network is the asset here, protecting how people experience outreach from you is the right instinct."

### 3. Reframe

Shift perspective without dismissing the concern. Introduce new information, context, or a different angle that makes the situation feel more manageable.

**Rules:**
- Do not contradict what you just acknowledged — build on it
- Use concrete specifics, not abstract reassurance
- If the concern cannot be fully solved, say so honestly and reframe the *risk magnitude* instead of pretending the problem doesn't exist
- Never say "don't worry about it" or equivalent

**Example:**
> Reframe: "the message tone reads natural in english even for international contacts — and if anyone replies in russian you just continue in russian, feels seamless from their side."

### 4. Ask

Close with a low-friction next step or an easy action they can take. This gives them control without creating a task or obligation.

**Rules:**
- The ask must cost them almost zero effort
- Do not frame it as "otherwise we proceed" — that's an ultimatum disguised as a question
- Do not create recurring work for them (e.g., "review every batch before it goes out") unless they explicitly request that level of control
- If no action is needed, end on a statement that closes the loop — don't force a question

**Example:**
> Ask: "if any specific names come to mind just send them over, i'll exclude them same day."

## Supporting Techniques

Use these within the AARA framework when the situation calls for it.

### Feel-Felt-Found

Best for: objections where social proof reduces anxiety.

**Structure:** "I get how that feels — [similar person/company] felt the same way, what they found was [outcome]."

**Rules:**
- Only use real or plausible references. Do not fabricate case studies.
- Keep it brief. One sentence for the "found" — don't turn it into a pitch.

### Isolate the Concern

Best for: when you suspect the stated objection isn't the real objection, or when someone raises multiple concerns at once.

**Structure:** "is [stated concern] the main thing, or is there something else behind it?"

**Rules:**
- Ask this genuinely, not as a closing technique
- If they reveal a deeper concern, restart AARA from the top with the real objection
- Never use "is that the only thing stopping you?" — it sounds like you're trying to trap them

### Reframe Risk Magnitude

Best for: concerns that are technically valid but disproportionately weighted vs. the upside.

**Structure:** Acknowledge the risk is real → quantify or contextualize how often it actually occurs → compare to the value being generated.

**Rules:**
- Only use when you have actual data or reasonable estimates
- Never dismiss low-probability concerns if the *impact* would be severe (e.g., legal, reputational with a key account)
- Let them draw the conclusion — present the context, don't tell them their concern is small

## Anti-Patterns

Do not do any of the following. These break trust.

| Anti-Pattern | Why It Fails |
|---|---|
| Jumping to solution before acknowledging | Person feels unheard, becomes more defensive |
| "I understand, but..." | "But" negates everything before it. They hear dismissal. |
| Creating work for the objector | If they raised a concern and your solution is to give them a task, you've punished them for speaking up |
| Defending the thing they're concerned about | They didn't ask for a defense. They asked to be heard. |
| "Don't worry about it" / minimizing | Implies they're wrong to feel what they feel |
| Ultimatum framing ("otherwise we...") | Feels like a threat. Creates adversarial dynamic. |
| Over-apologizing | Signals low confidence and makes small issues feel bigger |
| Solving a problem they didn't raise | Stay on their concern, not what you think the concern should be |

## Decision Logic

Use this to decide how to handle an objection before responding.

```
1. Is the concern valid?
   ├── YES → Acknowledge directly. Do not reframe as if it's not real.
   │         Can you solve it?
   │         ├── YES → AARA with concrete solution at step 4
   │         └── NO → AARA with honest "can't solve" + risk magnitude reframe
   └── NO / PARTIALLY → Acknowledge the underlying value (why they care),
                         then reframe with facts that shift perspective.

2. Is the stated concern the real concern?
   ├── UNSURE → Isolate before proceeding with AARA
   └── YES → Proceed with AARA

3. Is this high-stakes (reputation, legal, key relationship)?
   ├── YES → Do not reframe risk magnitude. Take it at face value.
   │         Offer maximum control/visibility to the objector.
   └── NO → Reframe risk magnitude if appropriate.

4. Who has the power dynamic?
   ├── THEY DO (client, CEO, buyer) → End on a zero-effort ask.
   │   Never create work for them. Never frame as "we proceed unless..."
   └── YOU DO (manager, advisor) → Can be slightly more direct at step 4.
```

## Tone Rules

- Match their register. If they're casual, be casual. If they're formal, be formal.
- Never be defensive. If they're frustrated, absorb it — don't reflect it back.
- Confidence without arrogance. You've looked into it, you have a view, you're sharing it.
- Short sentences. Especially on mobile-first channels (Slack, Telegram, LinkedIn DMs).
- End clean. Don't trail off with "let me know what you think?" unless you genuinely need their input to proceed. A clean closing statement > a hedging question.
