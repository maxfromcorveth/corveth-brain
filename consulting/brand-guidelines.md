## Introduction

This document establishes the visual identity system for Corveth Consulting, a revenue operations consulting firm dedicated to helping growth-stage B2B SaaS companies scale from $5M to $50M in ARR without operational chaos. The visual identity reflects the brand's core positioning: practical, action-oriented, and committed to implementation over assessment.

The brand personality strikes a careful balance between professionalism and approachability. It avoids the stiff, corporate feel of traditional consulting firms while maintaining the competence and credibility expected from experienced operators. The visual system should feel like a growth-stage company: modern, agile, capable, and ready to partner with ambitious businesses on their scaling journey.

## 1. Logo Design

### 1.1 Primary Logo Concept

The Corveth Consulting logo is built around the concept of "flight" and "direction" — the corveth (a intelligent, purposeful bird) represents strategic vision and forward momentum. The design communicates movement toward clearly defined outcomes, aligning with the brand's promise of staying until results are achieved.

The primary logo consists of two elements: a symbolic icon and a custom wordmark. The icon suggests an upward trajectory through a stylized wing or flight path, rendered in clean geometric forms. The wordmark uses a modified sans-serif typeface with confident letterforms that convey stability and professionalism without appearing rigid or stuffy.

The logo should always appear with adequate spacing and never be distorted, recolored beyond the approved palette, or combined with unauthorized elements. The primary logo works best in contexts where horizontal space permits, such as website headers, business cards, and formal documents.

### 1.2 Logo Variations

**Horizontal Logo (Primary)**
The horizontal configuration places the icon to the left of the wordmark, with proportional spacing between elements. This is the default version for most applications, including the website header, email signatures, and letterhead. The horizontal logo provides a professional appearance while maintaining balance and readability at various sizes.

**Stacked Logo**
The stacked version positions the icon above the wordmark, creating a compact vertical format. This variation is specifically designed for profile images, social media avatars, favicons, and other contexts where horizontal space is limited. The stacked version maintains identical proportion and spacing relationships, simply reoriented for vertical presentation.

**Icon-Only Mark**
For situations requiring maximum versatility, such as app icons, watermarks, or very small reproductions, the icon may be used independently without the wordmark. The icon-only mark requires the same clear space guidelines as the full logo and should only be used when the context makes the brand association immediately clear.

### 1.3 Clear Space and Minimum Size

**Clear Space**
Maintain a minimum clear space around all logo versions equal to the height of the icon element (or the letter "C" in the wordmark). This clear space ensures the logo remains uncluttered and prominent in any context. No other graphic elements, text, or visual matter should intrude into this protected zone.

**Minimum Size**
The primary horizontal logo should not be reproduced smaller than 32mm in width for print applications or 120 pixels for digital applications. The stacked version has a minimum width of 24mm print / 80 pixels digital. The icon-only mark should not appear smaller than 16mm print / 48 pixels digital. Below these thresholds, legibility and impact are compromised.

### 1.4 Logo Don'ts

The following practices violate brand guidelines and should never be applied to Corveth Consulting logos:

- Do not stretch, compress, or otherwise distort the logo proportions
- Do not rotate the logo or change its orientation
- Do not apply drop shadows, gradients, or 3D effects
- Do not change colors outside the approved palette
- Do not add decorative elements, borders, or frames
- Do not place the logo on busy or low-contrast backgrounds
- Do not recreate or modify the logo typefaces
- Do not separate the icon from the wordmark in unauthorized configurations
- Do not place the logo too close to other elements
- Do not use the logo at sizes below minimum thresholds

## 2. Color Palette

### 2.1 Primary Colors

The primary color palette establishes the brand's visual foundation and should dominate all brand applications. These colors communicate the brand's core personality: confident, capable, and approachable.

**Deep Navy (#1A2744)**
RGB: 26, 39, 68
This rich, dark navy serves as the primary brand color and primary text color. It conveys professionalism, trust, and stability — essential qualities for a consulting brand. Navy provides strong contrast against lighter tones and creates a sophisticated foundation for the palette. Use this color for headings, primary buttons, and key UI elements.

**Vibrant Teal (#00A896)**
RGB: 0, 168, 150
This energetic teal serves as the primary accent color and represents action, growth, and forward momentum. The color balances professionalism with energy, avoiding the sterile feel of traditional consulting blues while maintaining credibility. Use teal for call-to-action buttons, links, highlights, and accent graphics.

**Warm White (#F8F9FA)**
RGB: 248, 249, 250
A soft, warm white provides an approachable alternative to clinical pure white. This color serves as the primary background color and creates a welcoming, modern aesthetic. The slight warmth prevents the brand from feeling cold or corporate while maintaining cleanliness and clarity.

### 2.2 Secondary and Accent Colors

Secondary colors provide supporting roles in brand applications and offer variety while maintaining harmony with the primary palette.

**Slate Gray (#4A5568)**
RGB: 74, 85, 104
This medium gray provides a versatile secondary color for body text, supporting headlines, and UI elements that require less emphasis than primary colors. Slate gray offers excellent readability and pairs naturally with both navy and teal.

**Soft Gold (#D4A84B)**
RGB: 212, 168, 75
A warm, muted gold introduces a premium, accomplished feel without appearing ostentatious. Use sparingly for decorative accents, icon highlights, or elements that require a touch of distinction. Gold communicates success and achievement — appropriate for a brand that delivers results.

**Light Gray (#E2E8F0)**
RGB: 226, 232, 240
This light gray serves as a background color for secondary sections, borders, and dividers. It provides subtle separation between content areas without creating harsh contrast.

### 2.3 Background Colors

Background colors establish the visual environment for all brand communications. The approved background palette ensures consistency while providing appropriate options for different contexts.

| Color Name | Hex Code | RGB | Usage |
|------------|----------|-----|-------|
| Warm White | #F8F9FA | 248, 249, 250 | Primary background, website pages, presentations |
| Pure White | #FFFFFF | 255, 255, 255 | Cards, content boxes, print materials |
| Light Gray | #F1F5F9 | 241, 245, 249 | Secondary backgrounds, subtle contrast areas |
| Deep Navy | #1A2744 | 26, 39, 68 | Dark mode, footer sections, dramatic statements |

### 2.4 Color Usage Guidelines

Consistent color application strengthens brand recognition and ensures professional presentation across all touchpoints. The following guidelines govern appropriate color usage:

**Primary Color Usage**
Navy should dominate as the foundation color, appearing in approximately 60% of design compositions. Teal serves as the accent, appearing in approximately 25% of compositions. White and warm white backgrounds fill the remaining 15%, creating breathing room and visual balance.

**Color Hierarchy**
Establish clear visual hierarchy through strategic color placement. Navy conveys authority and importance for primary messages. Teal draws attention to interactive elements and calls to action. Gray provides supporting information without competing for attention.

**Background Selection**
Choose background colors based on context and medium. Warm white works best for digital interfaces and presentations. Pure white is preferred for print materials and formal documents. Light gray adds depth for section dividers and card backgrounds. Deep navy creates impact for hero sections and dramatic moments.

### 2.5 Accessibility Notes

All color combinations must meet WCAG 2.1 accessibility standards for contrast ratios. The following combinations are approved for text and interactive elements:

**Approved Contrast Combinations**
- Navy text on Warm White background: 14.5:1 ratio (exceeds AAA requirements)
- Navy text on Light Gray background: 11.2:1 ratio (exceeds AAA requirements)
- Teal text on Navy background: 4.6:1 ratio (meets AA requirements)
- White text on Navy background: 12.8:1 ratio (exceeds AAA requirements)

**Interactive Elements**
Buttons and links must maintain minimum 3:1 contrast against adjacent colors. Interactive states (hover, focus, active) should increase contrast rather than decrease it. Avoid using color alone to convey information; pair color changes with additional visual indicators such as underlines, icons, or text labels.

## 3. Typography

### 3.1 Primary Typeface

The primary typeface for headings and display text should balance modern sophistication with approachability. The selected typeface communicates competence without stiffness, aligning with the brand's personality as experienced but not stuffy.

**Recommended Primary Font: DM Sans**
DM Sans is a geometric sans-serif with friendly proportions and excellent versatility. It features a contemporary feel while remaining highly readable across all size and applications. The font's clean lines and subtle character provide a modern, tech-forward aesthetic appropriate for growth-stage SaaS companies.

**DM Sans Font Family**
- DM Sans Regular (400) — Body text, secondary information
- DM Sans Medium (500) — Subheadings, emphasized body text
- DM Sans Bold (700) — Headlines, primary calls to action

**Source and Availability**
DM Sans is available through Google Fonts (free, open source) and can be accessed at: https://fonts.google.com/specimen/DM-Sans

**Alternative: Plus Jakarta Sans**
If DM Sans becomes unavailable or requires licensing, Plus Jakarta Sans serves as an excellent alternative with similar geometric proportions and friendly character. Available at: https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Plus+Jakarta+Sans

### 3.2 Secondary Typeface

Body text requires a typeface optimized for extended reading and practical utility. The secondary font should complement the primary typeface while providing excellent readability at smaller sizes.

**Recommended Secondary Font: Source Sans 3**
Source Sans 3 is a high-quality sans-serif designed for reading and interface design. It offers excellent legibility at small sizes, making it ideal for body copy, descriptions, and lengthy content. The font's neutral character allows content to take center stage while maintaining professional presentation.

**Source Sans 3 Font Family**
- Source Sans 3 Regular (400) — Standard body text
- Source Sans 3 Medium (500) — Emphasized body text, buttons
- Source Sans 3 SemiBold (600) — Subheadings, navigation

**Source and Availability**
Source Sans 3 is available through Google Fonts (free, open source) and can be accessed at: https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Source+Sans+3

Establishing clear typographic hierarchy helps readers navigate content efficiently and reinforces brand consistency across all applications.

### 3.4 Typography Best Practices

Maintain typographic consistency through these guidelines:

**Line Length**
Optimal reading comfort requires line lengths between 50-75 characters. Avoid lines that extend beyond 80 characters or fall below 40 characters, as both extremes reduce readability and comprehension.

**Paragraph Spacing**
Separate paragraphs with clear spacing rather than indentation. Use 1.5x to 2x the font size as paragraph spacing to create visual breathing room and guide the reader through content.

**Alignment**
Left-align body text for optimal readability (ragged right is acceptable for headlines). Avoid justified text, which creates uneven word spacing and reduces readability. Center alignment should be reserved for headlines and very short text blocks.

**Tracking and Leading**
Default letter spacing works well for most applications. Increase tracking slightly (0.02em to 0.05em) for all-caps headlines to improve readability. Maintain line height at 1.5 to 1.7 for body text to ensure comfortable reading.

## 4. Visual Style

### 4.1 Imagery Style

Corveth Consulting imagery should reflect the brand's practical, action-oriented personality while appealing to growth-stage B2B audiences. The visual approach balances professional credibility with human approachability.

**Photography Style**
Select photographs that depict real people in authentic work environments rather than posed stock imagery. Look for images that communicate collaboration, problem-solving, and purposeful activity. Subjects should appear confident but not arrogant, focused but not tense. Prefer natural lighting where possible, avoiding harsh studio setups that feel corporate or sterile.

**Image Categories**
Appropriate photography includes: teams collaborating around whiteboards, professionals in modern office environments, individuals in focused work moments, and authentic client-consultant interactions. Avoid images that feel disconnected from real business challenges, such as generic handshakes, overly staged meeting scenes, or unrealistic technology close-ups.

**Illustration Style**
If illustrations are required, use a clean, modern style with geometric shapes and limited color palettes. Illustrations should support rather than dominate content, serving as visual explanations or conceptual representations. Flat or semi-flat styles work best, avoiding overly detailed or realistic illustrations that compete with content.

**Icon Style**
Icons should be line-based or simple solid shapes with consistent stroke weights (typically 1.5px to 2px). Use rounded line caps and corners for a friendly, approachable feel. Maintain consistent sizing and spacing across all icon applications. Icons should be monochromatic, using brand colors to indicate hierarchy or interaction states.

### 4.2 Graphic Elements

Graphic elements add visual interest and reinforce brand identity beyond typography and photography. These elements should be applied consistently to maintain professional presentation.

**Shape Language**
The brand utilizes soft rounded rectangles and organic shapes rather than sharp angles or aggressive geometry. This approach reinforces the approachable, friendly personality and avoids the cold precision often associated with consulting firms. Border radiuses between 8px and 16px create consistent visual language across UI elements.

**Dividers and Separators**
Use thin horizontal lines (1px) in Slate Gray or Light Gray to separate content sections. Alternatively, use generous whitespace as a natural divider, reducing visual clutter and maintaining the clean, modern aesthetic.

**Shadows and Depth**
Apply subtle shadows for depth and hierarchy rather than relying on borders. Use soft, diffused shadows with low opacity (5-10% black) and minimal blur (8px-16px). Shadows should enhance rather than dominate, maintaining the clean aesthetic.

**Accent Shapes**
Geometric accent shapes (circles, half-circles, organic blobs) may be used sparingly to add visual interest. Apply these elements at low opacity (5-15%) as background treatments, never as prominent foreground elements. Keep accent shapes in brand colors, primarily Navy and Teal.

### 4.3 Patterns and Textures

The brand should generally maintain a clean, uncluttered visual style. If patterns or textures are required, they should be subtle and minimal.

**Subtle Background Patterns**
Very faint geometric patterns (dots, lines, or grids) may be used at 3-5% opacity to add visual interest to large background areas. Patterns should never compete with content or reduce readability.

**Texture Guidelines**
Avoid heavy textures that add visual noise. If texture is desired for brand differentiation, use subtle grain or paper textures at very low opacity. Most applications benefit from flat, clean backgrounds rather than textured surfaces.

**Gradients**
Subtle gradients may be used for section backgrounds or accent elements, but should remain minimal. Recommended gradients include: Navy to slightly lighter navy (5-10% lightness change), or Teal to slightly darker Teal for button hover states. Avoid rainbow gradients, harsh color transitions, or gradients that introduce colors outside the approved palette.

## 5. Brand Applications

### 5.1 Business Cards

Business cards serve as a tangible brand touchpoint and should communicate professionalism while remaining memorable.

**Dimensions**
Standard US business card size: 3.5" × 2" (88.9mm × 50.8mm). For international compatibility: 85mm × 55mm.

**Layout**
The stacked logo (icon above wordmark) works best for business cards given the limited horizontal space. Position the logo at the top left or center-top. Place contact information below using Source Sans 3 at 9-10pt. Include name, title, phone, email, website, and optional LinkedIn URL.

**Color Applications**
Primary: Navy text on Warm White stock. Alternative: White text on Navy stock for a bold statement. Use Teal for hyperlinks and interactive-style elements. Keep the design clean with generous white space.

**Print Specifications**
Use 16pt cardstock with a soft matte finish for a premium feel. Avoid glossy coatings that appear cheap or corporate. Include a subtle watermark of the icon-only mark at 10% opacity in a corner for subtle branding.

### 5.2 Letterhead

Letterhead establishes professional correspondence standards for all official communications.

**Layout**
Header contains the horizontal logo at appropriate size (approximately 30mm width), positioned with standard margins. Footer includes minimal contact information and a thin horizontal divider line. Body area provides generous margins (minimum 20mm all sides) for professional appearance.

**Color**
Use Navy for header elements and primary text. Use Slate Gray for secondary information and footer content. Maintain Warm White background throughout.

**Typography**
Use Source Sans 3 for all letterhead text. Body text at 11-12pt with 1.5 line spacing. Name and title in Medium weight, contact details in Regular weight.

### 5.3 Social Media Avatars

Social media profiles require consistent visual identity across platforms with different aspect ratios and display sizes.

**Profile Images**
The icon-only mark (logo icon) serves as the primary profile image. Ensure the icon has adequate padding within the circular or square crop area of each platform. The icon should be Navy on transparent or White background for maximum versatility.

**Cover/Banner Images**
Banner images should use the brand color palette and typography rather than attempting to replicate the full logo at small sizes. Include the tagline "Revenue Operations Partner for Companies Ready to Scale" in DM Sans Bold with Navy or Teal text.

**Profile Information**
Use consistent bio text across platforms: "Revenue operations consulting for growth-stage B2B SaaS companies. We stay until it works." Link to website and include location.

### 5.4 Website Mood Board

The website should embody the brand's personality through thoughtful design that balances professionalism with approachability.

**Overall Aesthetic**
Clean, modern, and spacious. Generous white space creates breathing room and conveys confidence. Typography-driven design with clear hierarchy. Subtle animations for interactivity without distraction.

**Color Distribution**
Warm White backgrounds dominate, with Navy for primary text and Teal for interactive elements. Light Gray sections provide visual variety. Navy hero sections create impact for key moments.

**Content Presentation**
Content should be organized in clear, scannable sections. Use cards with subtle shadows for feature groupings. Include ample white space between sections. Prioritize clarity over density.

**Imagery Integration**
Authentic photography integrated naturally throughout. Team photos, workspace environments, and collaborative moments. Icons used sparingly to support but not clutter. Illustrations only where they add genuine value.

**Interaction Design**
Buttons with clear hover states in Teal. Smooth transitions between sections. Mobile-responsive layouts that maintain brand consistency. Loading states and micro-interactions that feel polished.
